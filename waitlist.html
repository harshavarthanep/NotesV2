The page

A page that behaves like paper, and a keyboard that never leaves your hands.

An A4 sheet with a draggable margin ruler, a rounded toolbar, and autosave that never asks. Everything else stays out of the way until you call it with a single character.

Type / and the page rearranges itself

Headings, bullets, numbered lists, checklists, quotes, code blocks, today’s date, the current time. Arrow keys to move, Enter to insert.

  • [[ opens a note picker — pick an existing note or create the missing one on the spot.
  • # autocompletes tags you already use, so your vocabulary stays consistent.
  • Paste a URL and it becomes a real link. Click it to open, edit or unlink.
  • Hover any paragraph on desktop for a ▲▼ handle that moves the whole block.
  • A ▸ chevron beside every heading folds that section away.

Autosave, then silence

Saving… → Saved, in the header. Opening or reading a note never writes anything, so nothing is touched by accident.

Notes on your notes

Select text, add a comment, and the passage is highlighted in gold. Resolve clears both the note and the highlight.

Outline that follows you

Your headings become a page navigator: the section you’re reading is highlighted, sub-sections collapse, and a search box filters them.

Images that stay light

Photos are resized and compressed as they go in, so notes keep syncing fast. Tap one for a full-screen viewer with pinch-zoom and download.

Task progress

Any note with checkboxes gets a progress bar above the page. The footer tracks words, characters, reading time, writing speed and flow.

A toolbar for thumbs

On a phone the toolbar can dock to the bottom and rides above the keyboard. One tap ticks a checkbox — no long press.

Backlinks, and the ones you haven’t made yet

Every note shows who links to it. Underneath, unlinked mentions hunt for notes that talk about this one without linking — the discovery step most tools skip.

  • Graph view Ctrl G — hold a node to spotlight its connections, pinch to zoom, drag to arrange. Node size grows with incoming links; colour follows the folder.
  • Tags live in the sidebar with counts. Tap one to filter, tap again for everything.
  • Search Ctrl K covers titles, full content and #tag queries — and runs off the main thread, so it stays instant with a thousand notes.
  • Reference panel Alt R pins a second note beside the editor, read-only, for side-by-side work.
From writing to doing

Six ways to look at the same notes.

Nothing is duplicated. A kanban card, a matrix row, a calendar entry and a canvas card are all the same note, seen from a different angle.

Home

Pinned and starred notes, every open task across the workspace, today’s note and a seven-day completion chart.

Daily notes

One tap opens today — priorities, notes and a reflection prompt, already laid out.

Calendar

A sun marks days with a daily note, a badge counts what you wrote. Tap a day for its notes, or jump to any month from 1950 to 2100.

Kanban board

To do · In progress · Done. Drag on desktop, use the arrows on a phone, and grow a card’s preview by dragging its corner.

Eisenhower matrix

Do first, Schedule, Delegate, Eliminate. Add a due date and time and the note reminds you — snooze ten minutes or an hour.

Canvas

Double-click empty space for an idea card. Connect two cards, group them in a frame, and the frame moves everything inside it.

Templates

Meeting, Project, Cornell, Weekly review — or save any note as one. Variables like {{date}} and {{title}} fill themselves in.

Quick view

The eye icon anywhere shows the note rendered exactly as in the editor, read-only. Escape closes one layer at a time.

Attention

Modes for when the work needs quiet.

Focus timer, zen mode, reading mode

  • Focus timer — 25, 45, 5, 15 minutes or any length you type. Close the panel and a small pill keeps the countdown in the corner; you get a notification when it ends.
  • Zen mode Ctrl . hides everything except the page.
  • Reading mode Ctrl E locks editing so you can read without nudging a word.
  • Read aloud speaks the note on-device, with speed control, pause and resume.
  • Reminders per note, with snooze, a system notification and a buzz on phones.

On-device intelligence

Study aids that never leave your machine.

No accounts to connect, no models to download, nothing sent anywhere. It reads the structure you already wrote.

Local summary & tags

Scores your sentences and pulls the few that carry the note, then suggests tags from the words you actually repeat. Insert at the top or copy.

Study mode

Turns headings, bold terms and lists into flashcards you can flip and shuffle — plus a guided walkthrough, section by section.

Deep Think map

A live structural map: sections sized by how much you wrote, with their links and task counts. Click a block to jump there.

Time machine

Snapshots as you write. Drag the slider and every word that differs from the current note is highlighted. Restore is undoable.

Your data

Written by you, kept for you, and easy to take away.

Offline by design

Pause syncing for a flight; keep writing. Ctrl S reconnects and pushes everything. A quiet reminder appears if you forget.

Trash, not gone

Deleted notes wait 30 days with a day counter, restorable in one tap, then clear themselves.

Locked notes

Password-protect a note. It still syncs and still backs up — and if the password slips your mind, confirm your account password to unlock it.

Backup & restore

Export every note and folder to one file. Importing adds notes and never overwrites what you already have.

Real Word files

A true .docx with your formatting intact — not an HTML file with the wrong extension.

PDF with real text

Paginated A4, selectable and searchable text, always on a white page whatever theme you use.

Markdown both ways

Export Obsidian-ready .md with #tags and [[links]] intact, or import .md and .csv files — several at once.

Publish a page

Turn any note into a standalone .html page you can host anywhere. Nothing to sign up for.

      Questions
      
Before you start.

    

    

      
Where are my notes stored?
In your own account in the cloud, with a local cache on each device. That cache is why the app opens instantly and keeps working through a dead connection — edits queue up and sync the moment you’re back.

      
Does it work without internet?
Yes. Switch on offline mode to pause syncing deliberately, or just lose signal — either way you keep writing. Ctrl S reconnects and pushes everything. Reminders and the focus timer fire while ZenDocs is open, including as a background tab or the installed app.

      
Is the on-device summary sending my notes anywhere?
No. The summary, the suggested tags, the flashcards and the Deep Think map are all computed in the app from the text you wrote. Nothing is uploaded for them.

      
Can I get my writing out again?
Always. Word (.docx), PDF with real selectable text, Markdown with your #tags and [[links]] intact, plain text, a standalone web page, or one backup file containing every note and folder.

      
How do I install it?
From the browser, on any device. Open ZenDocs, then use the browser menu — Install app on Android and Chrome, the install icon in the address bar on Edge, and Share → Add to Home Screen in Safari on iPhone and iPad. You get a full-screen app with its own icon and an offline shell. Packaged installers for Android and Windows are in testing and go to testers first.

      
Can I share a note with someone who doesn’t use ZenDocs?
Yes. Share creates a read-only link that anyone can open. They see the note live as you edit it, and can save a copy if they sign in. You can see who has opened a shared note in File details.

      
How long is the waiting list?
Invites go out in batches as testing progresses. You’ll get the download link and a short note on what to try first at the address you left.
